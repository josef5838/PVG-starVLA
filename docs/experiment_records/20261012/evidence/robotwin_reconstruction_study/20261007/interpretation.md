**Findings from the registered 12-case study**

The poor Surflo wrist surfaces recur across the tested RoboTwin tasks. All twelve comparisons are complete: rotate_qrcode, adjust_bottle, and stack_blocks_three, two source episodes per task, initial and deterministic gripper-interaction frames. All cases remain included. Each case has full PLY files, four-model source-camera renders, and an interactive mesh viewer. Policy training was not launched. The next Omega policy experiments are recorded in the [protocol](../../../docs/omega512_experiment_protocol_20261007.md).

Start with the [four-model wrist geometry sheet](wrist_geometry_overview.png), the [Surflo stage sheet](wrist_stage_overview.png), and the [case index](report.md).

| Method | Mean wrist RGB MAE | Mean wrist mesh coverage | Median relative wrist-camera rotation error |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Surflo + full default guidance | 0.3883 | 60.4% | 22.15° |
| VGGT | 0.0209 | 99.8% | 22.35° |
| Omega 256 Text | 0.0212 | 99.5% | 19.18° |
| Omega 512 | 0.0287 | 99.8% | 18.89° |

These are fitted-view diagnostics, not metric surface errors. Depth meshes are colored from their source images, and have dense grid connectivity; Surflo uses a shared generated representation and a different surface extraction method. Coverage means pixels reached by an extracted surface, not pixels with correct geometry. Neither coverage nor source-camera RGB error establishes precise object geometry or novel-view performance. This sample does not establish an overall ranking of the three depth backbones. Initial wrist images often contain almost exclusively low-texture wall/table regions; the interaction frames add close objects and substantial occlusion.

**The Gaussian and surface stages behave differently.** Surflo's average wrist Gaussian RGB MAE is 0.0377, with alpha coverage of 100%, whereas its mesh MAE is 0.3883. In all twelve cases the mesh fits wrist RGB worse than the Gaussian render and worse than each depth mesh. The shaded/normal figures show that this is accompanied by coarse facets, holes, and distorted surfaces. This is direct evidence that reproducing image colors through the guided Gaussian representation does not reliably produce usable surfaces under these conditions.

Two cases undergo especially severe collapse: rotate_qrcode episode 7 initial finishes with 73 full Gaussians and a 24-face mesh; stack_blocks_three episode 7 initial finishes with 118 Gaussians and a 22-face mesh. Both started with 100,000 queries. Their wrist mesh coverage is respectively zero and approximately 0.001%, despite full Gaussian alpha coverage. Rotate_qrcode episode 23 initial finishes with 226 Gaussians and only 14.6% mean wrist mesh coverage. The original logs retain the pruning/densification summaries; these are reconstruction outcomes, independent of the later visualization fix.

**The supplied BEHAVIOR example already fails before meshing.** Its guided Gaussian RGB MAE is 0.0077 for the head, 0.6120 for the left wrist, and 0.2000 for the right wrist. Inspect the [stage figure](behavior_renderer_audit/surflo_stage_diagnostics.png). Mesh recoloring can transfer input colors onto a distorted surface, which explains why mesh RGB can look closer than the Gaussian render while its geometry remains poor. Correcting the scalable renderer changed none of the original Surflo RGB, shading, or Gaussian image pixels: [pixel comparison](behavior_renderer_audit/renderer_effect.json). The original source result and the supplied comparison are preserved.

**This does not isolate 3DGS as the cause.** The installed Surflo model uses flow matching to generate oriented surface points. Its guided recipe additionally fits a Gaussian representation using RGB/depth/normal guidance and extracts a wrapping surface from those Gaussians. VGGT and Omega here triangulate depth grids instead. Several things change together: the learned scene prior, point generation, Gaussian fitting and pruning, predicted camera geometry, and surface extraction. The stage comparison locates a major degradation between appearance fitting and surface extraction; a controlled alternative extraction/representation would be needed to attribute it specifically to 3DGS. [Official Surflo description](https://anttwo.github.io/surflo/), [implementation](https://github.com/Anttwo/Surflo).

**Camera estimation is an additional concern.** Relative wrist rotations differ from the recorded rig by a median of about 19–22° across all four pipelines, with large camera-center direction errors. Surflo's small pose refinement does not repair this. Sparse overlap, close wrist views, occlusion, and low texture are plausible contributors to an underconstrained reconstruction, but this study does not causally isolate them from domain shift or the learned surface prior. Source HDF5 has camera calibration but no depth ground truth. Smooth depth meshes and attractive colors therefore do not demonstrate a correct common 3D scene.

**A real rendering defect was fixed, but it is a separate issue.** The scalable mesh renderer removed the first unique face index unconditionally, incorrectly assuming the first entry was always background. It also crashed when zero faces reached a pixel. The fix removes only an actual background sentinel and returns empty images for empty rasterization. Full-coverage, partial-coverage, offscreen, and empty-mesh CUDA checks passed. Every registered case was recolored/rerendered with the corrected code, without changing the saved Surflo reconstruction tensors or raw mesh geometry; per-case SHA256 assertions verify this. Original failures/outputs are retained. See [patch](mesh_renderer_fix.patch), [validation](renderer_validation.json), and [protocol amendment](renderer_protocol_amendment.json).

For policy work, these results support deferring Surflo mesh supervision. They do not prove that Surflo or Omega feature tokens lack useful visual information; that is what the registered visual-only localization probe and small varied-trajectory fitting experiment will test. To resolve reconstruction causes next, use the recorded camera rig in a consistently rebuilt backprojection/reconstruction, obtain simulator depth for metric object-region scoring, and independently control Gaussian pruning and the extraction procedure. Simply rendering the existing estimated-coordinate mesh with ground-truth cameras would not be a valid camera control.

All numeric data are in [summary.json](summary.json); execution and source provenance are in [manifest.json](manifest.json), [checkpoint hashes](checkpoint_hashes.json), the saved configurations, and each case's logs. Display viewers use decimated copies; PLY files retain the extracted geometry, including failed/degenerate results.
